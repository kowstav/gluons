# Gluons opportunities and upstream automation research

Research cutoff: **30 October 2026**. This is an internal product/research document, not customer marketing. Implementation and availability are described separately from market hypotheses.

## Finding

Start with **small field-service offices and property-maintenance administrators**, while continuing food/retail. Pilot vehicle-repair administration and small-fleet paperwork next. These businesses have recurring requests, receipts, stock counts and job records that can be prepared from digital inputs, checked, and approved. Do not offer autonomous physical work, diagnosis, freight dispatch, legal decisions or unrestricted agents.

“No AI use” is not a defensible description of an entire industry. The measurable opportunity is a population of non-adopters plus businesses using chat tools without integrating them into everyday work. No interviews, customer audits or production ROI measurements were performed for this report. Narrow-sector adoption claims below are explicitly hypotheses where the available surveys report only broad industries.

## October capability assessment

Use narrow tools before a reasoning model: PDF text extraction, OCR, CSV parsing, threshold checks, fuzzy lookup and date parsing already work locally. An agent can orchestrate these and draft an explanation, while trusted business facts control executable arguments. A larger model can help with ambiguous language; it cannot establish that an extracted amount is correct or that a technician is available.

[METR's September 2026 time-horizon page](https://metr.org/time-horizons/) measures software-task difficulty at specified success probabilities. Its [limitations note](https://metr.org/notes/2026-01-22-time-horizon-limitations/) cautions against interpreting a 50% horizon as permission to delegate all shorter real-world tasks. This report does not turn coding benchmarks into claims of autonomous business reliability. Local fixture tests below demonstrate bounded functionality, not field accuracy across vendors or languages.

## What is executable now

`/dashboard?tab=workflows` offers six paths. `/api/documents` reads a bounded owner upload; `/api/workflows` prepares and stages proposals through the existing router. Four new packs share two new core components, `job-intake` and `job-sheet`. All packs explicitly state practitioner validation is pending. Eight Agent Skills are exported. Existing saved businesses retain their enabled services; create a new business setup for new service combinations.

Text/PDF/CSV and retrieval/rules paths need no new account. OCR needs `GLUONS_OCR_ENABLED=true` on a compatible Node worker host. The invoice2data worker needs Python 3.12 and `GLUONS_INVOICE_PYTHON`; it is optional on hosts such as Vercel. Uploads are read in memory and not retained as files; approved task source text remains in the existing owner-scoped workspace. Hosted sign-in/storage requirements remain unchanged.

The prototype remains USD-only. English OCR and English date parsing are the tested defaults. This is not yet a deployment for Indian invoices, GST, multilingual calls, calendar availability, vendor accounting, POS, transport management or live tenant/repair operations. Those are explicit pilot-integration requirements.

## Pilot and cost measurement

Recruit 3–5 owner-operated businesses per first niche. Collect at least 50 representative tasks per workflow, with consent, while normal operations continue. A domain practitioner validates the sector rules once; setup staff then enter each business's catalog, hours, thresholds, timezone and exception routes. Measure baseline handling, review, corrections, missed urgent requests, field accuracy and completed outcomes. Do not use synthetic demo history as a customer baseline.

Per task, measure `manual minutes − review minutes − correction minutes − allocated setup/maintenance minutes`. Multiply positive net hours by that business's loaded hourly cost to estimate **time value**. Subtract all AI, OCR/CPU hosting, telephony, messaging, storage and support costs separately. Released capacity is not necessarily reduced payroll.

Illustrative sensitivity only: 220 monthly invoices × 3 manual minutes = 11 hours. If review/correction takes 1 minute each plus 2 hours of monthly support, net returned time is about 5.3 hours; at a hypothetical $20 loaded hourly rate, about $107 of time value **before all running fees**. At 3 minutes review/correction, the same workflow has no gross time advantage and support makes it negative. This is why an owner trial, not a generic savings percentage, decides viability.

Local parsing has zero model-token charge but nonzero compute/maintenance cost. OCR can be slower than a tiny model request and should run only when needed. Cheap/frontier AI pricing was not independently repriced in this research; the existing app's dated configurable pricing remains an estimate. Re-check provider rates before giving a commercial quote.

Advance a workflow only when its measured net time is positive, total cost fits that customer's value, the owner accepts the review burden, and its error/urgent-case behavior meets an agreed threshold. Reject workflows with frequent ambiguous totals, missing setup, tiny volumes or poor digital inputs. Do not infer layoffs or guaranteed savings from automation availability.
